### Walking routes and maps

Walking-route accuracy matters as much as visual quality.

- Verify venue names, addresses, coordinates, pedestrian connections, and the practical order of stops against reliable current sources.
- Check that the route follows actual walkable streets and bridge crossings; do not draw a decorative line that implies an impossible route.
- Use correct, sequential numbering. The map, itinerary, legend, and supporting text must agree.
- Show forward arrows following the intended walking direction, including at crossings and turns where needed.
- Label start and finish clearly. A five-stop itinerary must show five confirmed stops; distinguish any separate meeting marker from tour stops.
- Check numbering and arrows at mobile and desktop sizes, and in any printable version.
- Pair maps with a readable text itinerary. Do not rely on colour alone to communicate the route.
- Cite or retain verification sources and dates in project notes. Recheck time-sensitive details before launch.
- Do not claim step-free access or other accessibility characteristics without checking the actual route and venues.
